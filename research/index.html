Research

Connecting molecular layers to tissue biology

The program asks how molecular components interact to shape cell and tissue function. Spatial measurements can relate those components to cell state and tissue organization. Lung development and pediatric brain tumour biology are the two applications being built. This page does not claim that every molecular layer has already been measured.

Lung development and molecular organization

How do the molecular surroundings of cells change as the lung develops? We plan to integrate tissue imaging across molecular layers to study the organization of the airways, air sacs, supporting tissue and blood vessels. A complementary methods program will develop and evaluate approaches for combining measurements collected at different resolutions and from neighbouring tissue sections. Our goal is to understand how local molecular environments relate to gene activity and protein expression during development.

Pediatric brain tumours and their microenvironment

Our pediatric brain tumour program will investigate how tumour cells interact with immune cells, blood vessels and the surrounding tissue. Focusing on glioma and medulloblastoma, we aim to map the relationships among blood–brain barrier biology, metabolism and N-glycosylation—the sugar chains attached to proteins. By bringing these molecular layers together, we seek to understand features associated with disease progression and treatment response.

Treatment response and lasting health

We plan to combine spatial tissue profiling with experimental models to investigate how treatment remodels the tumour microenvironment and adjacent brain tissue. This work will also examine links between tumour biology, treatment-related injury and longer-term cognitive and cardiac outcomes. Connected with the University of Ottawa Brain–Heart Interconnectome, this direction seeks to bridge molecular changes in tissue with the lasting effects of childhood brain tumours and their treatment.

Tools that connect scales

Across these directions, we aim to develop reproducible ways to integrate imaging measurements while accounting for differences in spatial resolution and tissue sampling. We intend to share research data and analysis tools when appropriate, subject to ethics, consent and publication requirements.
