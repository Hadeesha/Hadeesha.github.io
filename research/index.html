Research

Connecting molecules to tissue biology

How do molecular components interact to shape cell and tissue function? Spatial measurements can relate those components to cell state, tissue organization and ultimately function.

Projects

  1. Pediatric brain tumors
  2. Brain–heart
  3. Lung development
  4. Multi-omic data integration

Pediatric brain tumors and their microenvironment

Pediatric high-grade brain tumors such as gliomas remain among the deadliest childhood cancers. We study how the tumor microenvironment shapes progression and response to treatment, using spatial multi-omic imaging of patient tumor tissues alongside preclinical models, with the goal of informing patient care and new therapies.

Brain–heart health after childhood brain tumors

Most children treated for a brain tumor survive, often with lasting effects on cognition and heart health. We ask whether the tissue removed at diagnosis, read together with years of clinical follow-up, can help identify the children most at risk and point to ways of protecting their long-term brain and heart health.

Lung development and molecular organization

The developing lung coordinates the maturation of airways, air sacs, supporting tissue and blood vessels. We image how its molecules, from transcripts and proteins to lipids, metabolites and glycans, are organized around each cell, and how that organization changes as the lung matures.

Multi-omic data integration

The lab’s projects all utilize multi-omic imaging, in which molecular layers are measured by different instruments, at different resolutions and often on neighboring tissue sections. We develop the computational methods that integrate these layers to better understand tissue biology.
